Research

Prediction is the thread that unites our work. We study how brains use it in perception and movement, and we build machine learning models that predict neural activity and behaviour.

Research areas

Prediction in perception and motor control

Brains act under delays and uncertainty, so perception and movement depend on predicting what comes next. We study this in people, mostly with non-invasive recordings (EEG, EMG, kinematics and speech), and in public datasets.

  • Predictive information in neural populations, including public recordings of mouse visual cortex.
  • Balance recovery and motor control, with kinematics, muscle activity (EMG) and EEG after perturbations.
  • Speech perception: linguistic priors, surprise and the encoding of articulatory features.
  • Interacting people: how brain activity and movement align across partners (dual-EEG).

Prediction in machine learning models

We build models that predict neural activity and behaviour. Latent variable models with learned dynamics, such as dynamics contrastive learning (DCL) and CEBRA, can simulate a system and show where it departs from expectation, a signal we call model surprise.

  • Learning objectives: which prediction-based objective best accounts for cortical representations, compared with reconstruction.
  • Hierarchical latent dynamics across timescales, with top-down predictions as control signals, tested on synthetic data where the answer is known.
  • Models of healthy cortical dynamics applied to micro-ECoG, to flag where activity departs from their predictions (a feasibility study for research on the tumour border).
