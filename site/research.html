Research

Prediction is the thread that unites our work. We measure how much predictive information neural populations carry, and we build models that predict neural activity and behaviour.

Research areas

Predictive information in neural populations

How much do neural populations know about their own future, and how is it encoded? We measure predictive information with variational estimators and test whether it is compressed into a few collective features, as reported in the retina. We use public datasets, such as calcium imaging of mouse visual cortex.

Latent dynamics of brain and behaviour

We build latent variable models with learned dynamics, such as dynamics contrastive learning and CEBRA, that predict neural activity and behaviour. These models can simulate the system and reveal where it departs from expectation, a signal we call model surprise. We apply them to dual-EEG studies of interacting people and to hierarchical systems that evolve on several timescales.

Motor control and balance

Brains act under delays, so the motor system must re-plan after a perturbation. We combine kinematics, muscle activity (EMG) and EEG to test whether muscular and cortical signals act as predictive control variables during balance recovery. We also study the timing of cortical and motor activity in visuomotor control and grasping.

Speech, language and perception

How do prior expectations shape what we hear? We study how linguistic priors, surprise and articulatory features are represented in brain signals, including in second-language speakers.

From prediction to the operating room

A model of healthy cortical dynamics can flag where activity departs from its predictions. With micro-ECoG recorded during neurosurgery, we test whether this marks the border of a tumour, as a feasibility study for research.
