Research

Our projects connect recordings of human movement, speech and brain activity with models and algorithms from machine learning.

Research areas

Human movement and motor control

How does the brain coordinate many muscles and joints? We record muscle activity (EMG) and body kinematics, including during walking on a narrow beam with perturbations. We use contrastive learning to identify motor synergies, the shared patterns that simplify whole-body movement.

Speech and language

How are speech sounds produced and learned? We model the movements of the articulators and the speech signal, and we study how articulatory features are encoded, including in second-language speakers.

Vision and brain signals

What structure do visual responses have? We analyse EEG recorded while people view images, and look for low-dimensional manifolds that describe how the brain represents what it sees.

Learning latent dynamics

How can we find the hidden variables behind complex recordings? We develop self-supervised and contrastive methods to learn hierarchical and multimodal latent dynamics, and to separate the information that different signals share from what is unique to each.
