How the brain computes, and what it predicts

We study how brains extract what is predictable about the world, the body and their own activity. We combine human recordings, public datasets and machine learning models. We are based at the University of Ferrara, Italy.

About the group

Our group puts neuroscience at the centre: the goal is to understand how the brain computes. Machine learning, new experiments and new recording devices serve that goal, as tools that make new neuroscience questions answerable.

The thread that unites our work is prediction. Brains act under delays and uncertainty, so extracting what is predictable about the future (of the world, of the body, of their own activity) is a candidate organising principle of neural computation.

Research

Prediction in perception and motor control

Perception and movement depend on predicting what comes next. We study this in people with non-invasive recordings (EEG, EMG, kinematics and speech), in balance recovery, speech perception and interacting people, and in public datasets of mouse visual cortex.

Prediction in machine learning models

We build latent variable models with learned dynamics that predict neural activity and behaviour, and use them to test which learning objectives account for cortical representations, how hierarchies of timescales can be identified, and where activity departs from prediction.
