Join us

We offer computational master's thesis projects in neuroscience and machine learning, and we welcome enquiries about PhD and postdoctoral positions.

Master's thesis projects 2026/27

There are five computational projects, each lasting 9 to 12 months. They use public or synthetic datasets, except the micro-ECoG project, which uses data recorded by the lab. Each project has one question and a staged plan with a checkpoint at the end of every stage, so a complete thesis never depends on the riskiest step.

You will work in the lab, meet your supervisors regularly, keep a reproducible code repository and a work log, and read and discuss the key papers in the first two months. You need solid Python, basic machine learning, linear algebra and statistics, interest in neuroscience, and comfort reading and writing in English. PyTorch can be learned in the first month.

Latent dynamics of human balance recovery

Do muscle and cortical activity reduce a latent model's surprise after perturbations? A model learned from kinematics alone predicts balance recovery, and we test whether EMG and EEG, added as inputs, improve it in a specific and robust way.

Data: Public multimodal dataset with EEG, EMG and 3D kinematics from 30 participants.

Supervisors: Alessandro Corsini and Gerôncio Oliveira

Predictive information in mouse visual cortex

Is predictive information collectively and compressibly encoded in mouse visual cortex? In the retina, it is compressed into a few collective features. We test whether the same holds in V1 and higher visual areas.

Data: MICrONS functional data (calcium imaging, 14 scans).

Supervisors: Alessandro Corsini and Yirga Tikuye

Learning objectives and cortical responses

Which learning objective best accounts for visual cortical representations? We compare self-supervised models trained with reconstruction, information maximisation and latent-space prediction, under matched architecture, data and training budget.

Data: MICrONS functional data and the stimulus videos.

Supervisors: Alessandro Corsini and Yirga Tikuye

Hierarchical latent dynamics across timescales

Does top-down predictive control help identify multi-timescale systems? We test whether a hierarchy of dynamics contrastive learning models recovers slow and fast latent dynamics better than flat models.

Data: Synthetic system with known ground truth; real recordings as an extension.

Supervisors: Alessandro Corsini and Chang Hui Simone Lin

Tumour-border mapping from micro-ECoG

Can latent dynamics of spontaneous cortical activity map the tumour border, electrode by electrode? A model of healthy cortical dynamics flags where activity departs from its predictions. This is a feasibility study for research, not a clinical tool.

Data: Intraoperative micro-ECoG recorded by the lab, with healthy, tumour and border sessions.

Supervisors: Alessandro Corsini and Luca Carrara
